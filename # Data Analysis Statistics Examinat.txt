### **Q1. Variables**

**(a) Identify the type of variables.**

- Course – **Nominal:** Categories without any specific order.
- Satisfaction – **Ordinal:** Categories with a meaningful order or ranking.
- Assignments Completed – **Discrete:** Countable numerical values.
- Task Completion Time – **Continuous:** Measurable values that can include decimals.

**(b) Why should Student IDs not be used to calculate the mean?**

Student IDs are unique identification numbers. They do not represent quantity or have any mathematical order. Therefore, calculating their mean has no meaningful interpretation.

### **Q2. Population and Sample**

**(a) Identify the population and sample.**

- Population: All 240 Data Analytics students.
- Sample: 30 selected top students.

**(b) Identify the type of statistics.**

Inferential Statistics – We use sample data to draw conclusions about the entire population.

**(c) Is the sample biased? Explain.**

Yes, the sample is biased because only the top 30 students were selected. Their average score may be higher than the average score of all 240 students.

### **Q3. Mean, Median and Mode**

Given data: 12, 15, 15, 18, 40

**(a) Calculate Mean, Median and Mode.**

- Mean = Sum of values / Number of values
- Mean = 100 / 5 = **20**
- Median = Middle value = **15**
- Mode = Most frequently occurring value = **15**

**(b) Which measure is the best representation of typical time?**

Median (15) is the best measure because 40 is an outlier that increases the mean. The median represents the typical completion time more accurately.

Given data: 2, 4, 4, 6

**(a) Calculate the mean.**

Mean = (2 + 4 + 4 + 6) / 4

Mean = 16 / 4 = **4**

**(b) Calculate the deviations and squared deviations.**

| Value     | Deviation (x − Mean) | Squared Deviation |
| --------- | -------------------- | ----------------- |
| 2         | -2                   | 4                 |
| 4         | 0                    | 0                 |
| 4         | 0                    | 0                 |

**(c) Calculate Population Variance and Population Standard Deviation.**

Population Variance = 8 / 4 = **2**

Population Standard Deviation = √2 = **1.4142**

**(d) Calculate Sample Variance.**

\= 8 / 3 = **2.6667**

### **Q5. Quartiles and Outliers**

Given data: 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 30

Number of observations (n) = 8

**(a) Calculate Q1 and Q3.**

Q1 Position = (n − 1) × 0.25

\= 7 × 0.25 = 1.75

Q1 = 6 + 0.75 × (7 − 6) = **6.75**

Q3 Position = 7 × 0.75 = 5.25

Q3 = 10 + 0.25 × (12 − 10) = **10.5**

**(b) Calculate Interquartile Range (IQR).**

IQR = Q3 − Q1

\= 10.5 − 6.75 = **3.75**

**(c) Calculate the lower and upper fences.**

Lower Fence = Q1 − 1.5 × IQR

\= 6.75 − 5.625 = **1.125**

Upper Fence = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR

\= 10.5 + 5.625 = **16.125**

**(d) Identify the outlier.**

30 is an outlier because it is greater than the upper fence (16.125).

**(e) Identify the lower and upper whiskers.**

- Lower Whisker = **4**
- Upper Whisker = **12**

The value 30 is excluded because it is an outlier.

### **Q7. Covariance and Correlation**

Given:

- X = 1, 2, 3, 4
- Y = 20, 40, 60, 80

**(a) Calculate the mean of X and Y.**

Mean of X = (1 + 2 + 3 + 4) / 4 = **2.5**

Mean of Y = (20 + 40 + 60 + 80) / 4 = **50**

**(b) Calculate deviations and their products.**

| X | Y  | X Deviation | Y Deviation | Product |
| - | -- | ----------- | ----------- | ------- |
| 1 | 20 | -1.5        | -30         | 45      |
| 2 | 40 | -0.5        | -10         | 5       |
| 3 | 60 | 0.5         | 10          | 5       |
| 4 | 80 | 1.5         | 30          | 45      |
|   |    |             | **Total**   | **100** |

**(c) Calculate Sample Covariance.**

Sample Covariance = 100 / (4 − 1)

\= 100 / 3 = **33.3333**

**(d) Calculate the sample variances of X and Y.**

Variance of X = 5 / 3 = **1.6667**

Variance of Y = 2000 / 3 = **666.6667**

**(e) Calculate the correlation coefficient.**

\\[ r=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{S_X S_Y} \\]

\\[ r=\frac{33.3333}{\sqrt{1.6667\times666.6667}} \\]

Answer: **r = +1.0**

Interpretation: There is a perfect positive correlation between X and Y. When X increases, Y also increases proportionally.

### **Q8. Correlation – Conclusions**

**(a) Which correlation is stronger: +0.60 or −0.85?**

**−0.85 is stronger** because its absolute value (0.85) is greater than 0.60. The negative sign indicates an inverse relationship.

**(b) If correlation is zero, does it mean there is no relationship?**

No. It means there is no linear relationship. A non-linear relationship may still exist, such as a parabolic relationship (Y = X²).

**(c) What happens when one column has a constant value?**

Correlation is **undefined** because the variance is zero, resulting in division by zero.

**(d) Does correlation prove causation?**

No. Correlation does not imply causation. Other factors, such as talent, interest or experience, may influence the relationship.

### **Q10. Probability Calculations**

**(a) Binomial Distribution**

Given:

\\[ X\sim Binomial(4,0.5) \\]

**(i) Calculate P(X = 2).**

\\[ P(X=2)=\binom{4}{2}(0.5)^2(0.5)^2 \\]

\\[ =6\times0.0625 \\]

Answer: **0.375**

**(ii) Calculate P(X ≥ 1).**

\\[ P(X\geq1)=1-P(X=0) \\]

\\[ =1-0.0625 \\]

Answer: **0.9375**

**(b) Normal Distribution**

Given:

Mean (μ) = 50

Standard Deviation (σ) = 5

**(i) Calculate P(Y greater than 60).**

\\[ Z=\frac{60-50}{5}=2 \\]

\\[ P(Y>60)=1-\Phi(2) \\]

\\[ =1-0.9772499 \\]

Answer: **0.0227501**

**(ii) Calculate P(45 less than Y less than 55).**

\\[ Z_1=\frac{45-50}{5}=-1 \\]

\\[ Z_2=\frac{55-50}{5}=1 \\]

\\[ P(45\<Y<55)=\Phi(1)-\Phi(-1) \\]

Answer: **0.6826894**

**(c) Uniform Distribution**

Given:

\\[ T\sim U[10,20] \\]

**(i) Calculate the probability density at T = 15.**

\\[ f(15)=\frac{1}{20-10} \\]

Answer: **0.1**

**(ii) Calculate P(12 ≤ T ≤ 16).**

\\[ P(12\leq T\leq16)=\frac{16-12}{20-10} \\]

Answer: **0.4**
